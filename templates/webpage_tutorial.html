How to use LAGER

LAGER works backwards from the molecule you want (the target) to molecules you can buy or have in the lab (the starting materials). In each step it applies a reaction rule in the retrosynthetic direction, breaking a molecule into simpler precursors, until everything is a starting material. It ranks the pathways by the number of steps and by how easy the intermediates are to make (synthetic accessibility score).

The page has four steps, from left to right in the tabs at the top. This tutorial goes through them with an example.

Example target: a hydroxamic acid made from 3,4-dichloroaniline

Fills in the target in step 1. Then click Find pathways.

The page at a glance

{{ figure("overview.png", "The tabs at the top and the bar at the bottom of the page") }}
  1. Help: this tutorial (0a) and the guide to writing reaction rules (0b).
  2. The four steps. Each tab shows a short summary of its step, e.g. the target SMILES or how many reaction rules are used. You can check everything before you start the search.
  3. The bar at the bottom is always visible and shows what will be searched.
  4. Find pathways starts the search. You can click it from any tab.

1 Target molecule

{{ figure("target.png", "Step 1: molecule editor and SMILES field") }}
  1. Draw the target in the molecule editor (JSME).
  2. Or paste a SMILES. Drawing fills in this field automatically, and this field is what is searched. A pasted SMILES is not drawn in the editor.

2 Starting materials

{{ figure("materials.png", "Step 2: list of starting materials and upload") }}
  1. Which molecules count as available: the extended inventory (building blocks from MolPort) or the small inventory (a subsample of it, used by default).
  2. Upload your own instead: a .csv with a smiles column or an .sdf file, e.g. exported from a chemical inventory. Several files are combined. Salts are split into their parts, protonated amines neutralized, and stereochemistry, isotope labels and duplicates removed. SDF entries without a structure are looked up on PubChem by name or CAS number (only these are sent, never your target).

The example works with the default list.

3 Reaction rules

{{ figure("rules.png", "Step 3: rule list with folders and a rule picture") }}
  1. Search the rules by name.
  2. Folders group related rules (at first: all protections, all deprotections, rules whose names start with the same word). The tick box switches all rules of the folder on or off; click the name to open the folder. You can rename folders and move a rule to another folder (in the rule panel). Your ticks and folders are kept in your browser; Save setup to file keeps them as a .json file that you can load again later or on another computer.
  3. A single rule: tick it to use it in the search, click its name to see it.
  4. Switch between the retrosynthetic rule (used by the search) and the forward reaction (used to check for side reactions with other groups of the molecule).
  5. The rule picture: left side, right side and the unchanged context. Bonds that break are red, bonds that form are green; atoms are coloured by element. Click an atom to highlight it.
  6. Add your own rule, written by hand or created from example reactions. See the .

For the example, keep all rules switched on.

4 Results

After Find pathways, the page switches to the results tab and shows the progress (the example takes only a few seconds):

{{ figure("progress.png", "Step 4: the search running") }}
  1. The status: waiting, searching, done (or what went wrong).
  2. The search log: how many pathways were found and after how many iterations. Searches take from seconds to a few minutes and are stopped after 15 minutes.

When it is finished, the best pathways are shown (up to three), the best first:

{{ figure("results.png", "Step 4: a pathway found for the example") }}
  1. Download all (ZIP): every pathway as picture and data, plus the starting materials and reaction rules that were used, so the search can be repeated.
  2. The pathway, read from left to right: the target, each retrosynthetic step with the name of its rule, and the precursors. Green boxes are starting materials you have (buyable), grey boxes are intermediates that are made in an earlier step. For the example, LAGER suggests making the amide from methyl malonyl chloride and 3,4-dichloroaniline (Schotten–Baumann), then turning the methyl ester into the hydroxamic acid with hydroxylamine. A “Warning! … side reactions detected” means that the forward rules found other groups in the molecule that could react under the same conditions – check these steps. g, h and f are the search scores (cost so far, estimated remaining cost, and their sum).
  3. Download this pathway as a picture (PNG) or as data (JSON).

No pathway found? Check that the rules you need are switched on (step 3) and that the starting materials are in your list (step 2). If a reaction you need is missing, add it as a rule.
